1. Switchboard Sizer

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 - Electrical installations (Wiring Rules)
  • AS/NZS 61439.1 - Low-voltage switchgear and controlgear assemblies (supersedes AS/NZS 3439.1)

Main Switch Rating Calculation

Main Switch Rating = Total Connected Load × Diversity Factor × Safety Factor Where: - Total Connected Load = Sum of all circuit breaker ratings - Diversity Factor = User-defined (typically 0.6-0.8 for residential, 0.8-1.0 for commercial) - Safety Factor = 1.25 (25% margin as per AS/NZS 3000)
Total Connected Load
Sum of all individual circuit ratings (in Amps)
Diversity Factor
Acknowledgment that not all circuits will be at full load simultaneously. Lower values indicate greater diversity.
Safety Factor
25% overhead to prevent nuisance tripping and allow for future expansion

Available Main Switch Sizes

Standard ratings rounded up to next available size: 63A, 80A, 100A, 125A, 160A, 200A, 250A, 315A, 400A, 500A, 630A, 800A

Busbar Sizing

Busbar Current Capacity = Main Switch Rating Width Selection: - ≤125A: 200mm width (standard) - >125A-250A: 300mm width - >250A-400A: 400mm width - >400A-630A: 600mm width - >630A: 800mm width
Note: Busbar sizing follows manufacturer specifications and AS/NZS 61439.1 for current density and temperature rise limits (typically 30–50K rise at rated current).

2. Cable Sizing Calculator

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3008.1.1:2017 — Selection of cables (current-carrying capacity, voltage drop, short-circuit)
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 cl. 3.6.2 — voltage drop limit (5% from the point of supply to any point)
  • IEC 60364-5-52 — reference installation methods used for the indicative rating table
  • IEC 60228 — conductor resistance at 20°C

Current Carrying Capacity

Iz = Itab × ktemp × kgroup × nparallel  ≥  IB ktemp = √[(θc − θa) / (θc − θref)] θc = 90°C (X-90) or 75°C (V-90); θref = 30°C air, 20°C ground
Tabulated ratings (indicative)

Cu, three loaded conductors, XLPE/90°C, IEC 60364-5-52 reference methods A1, A2, B1, B2, C, D and E. PVC ratings are taken as 84% and aluminium as 78% of the copper XLPE values. Final selection must be confirmed against AS/NZS 3008.1.1 tables for the actual cable and installation.

Grouping factor (bunched circuits)
  • 1: 1.00   2: 0.80   3: 0.70   4: 0.65   5: 0.60   6: 0.57   7: 0.54   8: 0.52   9: 0.50   10–12: 0.45   13–16: 0.41   17–20: 0.38

Voltage Drop Calculation

Vd = k × (IB / n) × L × (R75 cosφ + X sinφ) k = 2 (1-phase), √3 (3-phase) R75 = R20 × [1 + α(75 − 20)], α = 0.00393 (Cu), 0.00403 (Al) Budget for this cable = min(user limit, 5% − upstream drop already used) (AS/NZS 3000 cl. 3.6.2)

Short-Circuit Withstand (adiabatic)

S ≥ I √t / k k = 143 (Cu X-90), 115 (Cu V-90), 94 (Al X-90), 76 (Al V-90)

Cable Selection Process

  1. For 1 run (then 2, 3, 4 runs in Auto mode), step through sizes 1.5–300 mm²
  2. Accept the first size that meets current rating, voltage-drop budget and short-circuit withstand together
  3. Report which criterion governed; report "no solution" rather than an undersized cable when nothing complies
Example: 32 A, 50 m, 3-phase 400 V, pf 0.9, Cu X-90 multicore in conduit on a wall (B2), 40°C, 3 circuits grouped

ktemp = √(50/60) = 0.913; kgroup = 0.70 → derating 0.639
6 mm²: 44 × 0.639 = 28.1 A < 32 A ✗;  10 mm²: 60 × 0.639 = 38.3 A ✓
R75 = 1.83 × 1.216 = 2.225 mΩ/m; Z = 2.225×0.9 + 0.090×0.436 = 2.042 mΩ/m
Vd = √3 × 32 × 50 × 2.042 / 1000 = 5.66 V = 1.41% ✓ → 10 mm²

3. Voltage Drop (Quick Calc and Cable Schedule)

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Clause 3.6.2 (5% from the point of supply to any point)
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Appendix C, Table C8 (simplified method, Am per %Vd)

Table C8 Method (Cable Schedule)

Vd (%) = I × L / (Am per %Vd) I = current (A), L = route length (m) Copper, Am per %Vd (single-phase 230 V / three-phase 400 V): 1.5 mm²: 70 / 140 2.5: 128 / 256 4: 205 / 412 6: 306 / 615 10: 515 / 1034 16: 818 / 1643 25: 1289 / 2588 35: 1773 / 3560 50: 2377 / 4772 70: 3342 / 6712 95: 4445 / 8927

Impedance Method (Quick Calc, larger sizes)

Vd = k × I × L × Z / 1000 k = 2 (1-phase), √3 (3-phase), Z in mΩ/m at operating temperature

Limits (Clause 3.6.2)

Total: 5% of nominal voltage between the point of supply and any point in the installation Distributed loads on final subcircuits (socket-outlets, lighting): current may be taken as half the protective device rating Consumer mains / submains / final subcircuit split is a design choice within the 5% total
Example (Table C8): 50 A over 75 m with 2.5% allowed → 50 × 75 / 2.5 = 1500 Am per %Vd → single-phase 35 mm² (1773), three-phase 16 mm² (1643).

4. Conduit Sizer

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 - Appendix C, Paragraph C6 and Tables C10–C12 (number of cables in conduit)
  • AS/NZS 2053 - Conduits and fittings for electrical installations

Cable Fill Calculation

Fill Ratio = (Total Cable CSA) / (Conduit Internal CSA) AS/NZS 3000:2018 Appendix C Paragraph C6.2 space factors: - Single cable: 50% maximum fill - 2 cables: 33% maximum fill - 3+ cables: 40% maximum fill
Cable Cross-Sectional Area
Overall diameter including insulation and sheath:
  • 1.5mm² TPS: ~6.5mm diameter → 33.2mm² CSA
  • 2.5mm² TPS: ~7.5mm diameter → 44.2mm² CSA
  • 4mm² TPS: ~8.5mm diameter → 56.7mm² CSA
  • 6mm² TPS: ~10mm diameter → 78.5mm² CSA
  • Single core PVC: Calculate from (conductor + 2×insulation thickness)²×π/4
Conduit Internal Area (AS/NZS 2053 medium-duty rigid PVC)
Based on actual internal diameter (OD minus wall thickness):
  • 20mm OD (ID ≈ 16.1mm):  ~204mm² internal area
  • 25mm OD (ID ≈ 21.2mm):  ~353mm² internal area
  • 32mm OD (ID ≈ 28.0mm):  ~616mm² internal area
  • 40mm OD (ID ≈ 35.4mm):  ~984mm² internal area
  • 50mm OD (ID ≈ 44.2mm): 1534mm² internal area
Note: Always use manufacturer data for exact wall thickness. Nominal OD does not equal ID.

Conduit Selection Process

  1. Count total number of cables to be installed
  2. Determine fill limit: 50% for 1 cable, 33% for 2 cables (AS/NZS 3000 Paragraph C6.2), 40% for 3+ cables
  3. Calculate total cable CSA (sum of all individual cable CSAs)
  4. Select smallest conduit where: Internal Area × Fill Limit ≥ Total Cable CSA
Example: 5 × 2.5mm² TPS cables (44.2mm² each)

Total cable CSA = 5 × 44.2mm² = 221mm²
Fill limit = 40% (3+ cables)
Required conduit area = 221mm² / 0.40 = 552.5mm²
Selected: 40mm conduit (ID 35.4mm, 984mm² internal area) ✓

5. Cable Weight Calculator

References:
  • Manufacturer specifications and datasheets
  • Material density calculations (Copper: 8.96 g/cm³, PVC: 1.4 g/cm³)

Weight Calculation Method

Cable Weight (kg/m) = (Conductor Weight + Insulation Weight + Sheath Weight) × Number of Cores Conductor Weight = CSA (mm²) × Length (m) × Copper Density (8.96 g/cm³) / 1000 Where CSA is the copper conductor cross-sectional area

Typical Cable Weights (kg/m)

Single Core PVC 0.6/1kV Copper
  • 1.5mm²: 0.028 kg/m
  • 2.5mm²: 0.041 kg/m
  • 4mm²: 0.059 kg/m
  • 6mm²: 0.082 kg/m
  • 10mm²: 0.133 kg/m
  • 16mm²: 0.206 kg/m
  • 25mm²: 0.318 kg/m
  • 35mm²: 0.437 kg/m
TPS (Twin & Earth) Copper
  • 1.5mm² TPS: 0.11 kg/m (2×1.5mm² + 1mm² earth)
  • 2.5mm² TPS: 0.17 kg/m (2×2.5mm² + 1.5mm² earth)
  • 4mm² TPS: 0.26 kg/m (2×4mm² + 2.5mm² earth)
  • 6mm² TPS: 0.37 kg/m (2×6mm² + 2.5mm² earth)

Cable Drum Load Calculation

Total Weight = Cable Weight per Meter × Drum Length + Drum Weight Typical drum weights: - Small drums (≤200m): 5-10 kg - Medium drums (200-500m): 10-20 kg - Large drums (500-1000m): 20-40 kg

6. Switchroom Sizer

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 - Clause 2.10 (Switchboards - location, access and egress)
  • AS/NZS 61439 (supersedes AS/NZS 3439.1) - Low-voltage switchgear assemblies (Clearance requirements)

Minimum Room Dimensions

Width = Equipment Width + 2 × Side Clearance (min 600mm each side) Depth = Equipment Depth + Front Access (min 1000mm) + Rear Clearance (min 600mm) Height = Equipment Height + Top Clearance (min 200mm) + Floor Mounting (min 150mm)
Front Working Space (AS/NZS 3000)
  • Normal access: 1000mm minimum
  • Restricted access: 700mm (only if egress available at both ends)
  • Service/maintenance access: 1200mm recommended
Side Clearances
  • For opening doors: 600mm minimum
  • For hinged panel access: 450mm minimum
  • Wall-mounted (no access): 50mm minimum
Rear Clearances
  • Rear access required: 800mm minimum
  • Cable entry only: 300mm minimum
  • No rear access: 150mm (for ventilation)
Ceiling Height
  • Standard switchboards: 2400mm minimum room height
  • Free-standing panels: Equipment height + 500mm minimum
  • Cable overhead runway: Add 600mm

Ventilation Requirements

Heat Dissipation (kW) = Switchboard Rating (kVA) × 0.02 to 0.03 Ventilation Area (m²) = Heat Dissipation (kW) / (Air Flow Rate × Temperature Rise) Typical values: - Natural ventilation: 0.5-1 m/s air flow - Forced ventilation: 2-4 m/s air flow - Temperature rise limit: 10-15K above ambient
Example: 630A main switch, 800mm wide switchboard

Width = 800mm + 2×600mm = 2000mm (2.0m)
Depth = 400mm (typical) + 1000mm + 600mm = 2000mm (2.0m)
Height = 2200mm + 200mm = 2400mm minimum ceiling
Room size: 2.0m × 2.0m × 2.4m high

7. Emergency Lighting Spacing

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 2293.1:2018 - Emergency escape lighting (System design, installation and operation)
  • AS/NZS 1680.0 - Interior and workplace lighting

Spacing Calculation (Lumen Method)

Maximum Spacing (m) = (Lumens × UF × MF) / (Required Lux × Width) Where: - Lumens = Luminaire output (from manufacturer data) - UF = Utilization Factor (0.4 to 0.6, depends on reflectance) - MF = Maintenance Factor (0.8 for standard LED emergency lights) - Required Lux = 0.2 lx minimum (AS/NZS 2293.1); higher design targets for car parks / high-risk areas - Width = Corridor or area width (meters)
Utilization Factor (UF)
Accounts for light lost to walls/ceiling absorption:
  • Light surfaces (70% reflectance): UF = 0.5-0.6
  • Medium surfaces (50% reflectance): UF = 0.4-0.5
  • Dark surfaces (30% reflectance): UF = 0.3-0.4
Maintenance Factor (MF)
Accounts for aging and dirt accumulation:
  • LED luminaires, regular cleaning: MF = 0.8
  • LED luminaires, poor maintenance: MF = 0.7
Required Illuminance (AS/NZS 2293.1)
  • Escape routes and open areas: 0.2 lx minimum horizontal at floor level (AS/NZS 2293.1)
  • Covered car parks: 1.0 lx used as a design target
  • High risk task areas: 10 lx design target, point-by-point design required
  • Classification mode (C/D classes) uses the AS/NZS 2293.1 spacing tables directly, without scaling

Mounting Height Constraint

Maximum Spacing ≤ 3 × Mounting Height This prevents excessive spacing that would create dark spots even if average illuminance is adequate. Based on photometric distribution patterns.
Example: 400 lumen LED, 2.5m high, 2m wide corridor, medium reflectance

UF = 0.5, MF = 0.8, Required = 0.2 lux
Spacing = (400 × 0.5 × 0.8) / (0.2 × 2) = 400 m (average-illuminance result)
Height constraint = 3 × 2.5m = 7.5m
Final spacing: 7.5m (limited by height — the lumen method averages light and cannot guarantee the minimum point illuminance; use classification tables or photometry) ✓

8. PV System Sizing

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 5033:2021 - Installation and safety requirements for photovoltaic arrays
  • AS/NZS 4777.1 - Grid connection of energy systems via inverters
  • Clean Energy Council Design Guidelines

Array Sizing Calculation

System Size (kW) = Daily Energy Use (kWh) / (Peak Sun Hours × System Efficiency) Where: - Daily Energy Use = Average daily consumption (kWh/day) - Peak Sun Hours = Location-specific (typically 4-5 hours in Australia/NZ) - System Efficiency = 0.75-0.80 (accounts for all losses)

Number of Panels

Number of Panels = System Size (W) / Panel Wattage (W) Round up to nearest whole number Example: 6500W system ÷ 400W panels = 16.25 → 17 panels

Inverter Sizing

Inverter Size = Array Size × (0.8 to 1.0) Ratio considerations: - 0.8-0.85: For hot climates or north-facing - 0.9-0.95: Standard residential - 1.0-1.1: For optimal orientation and cool climates
System Efficiency Factors
Overall efficiency (~75-80%) includes:
  • Inverter efficiency: 96-98%
  • DC cable losses: 2-3%
  • Temperature losses: 10-15%
  • Shading and soiling: 2-5%
  • Mismatch losses: 1-2%
Peak Sun Hours by Region
  • Northern Australia: 5.0-5.5 hours
  • Central Australia: 4.5-5.0 hours
  • Southern Australia: 4.0-4.5 hours
  • New Zealand North: 4.0-4.5 hours
  • New Zealand South: 3.5-4.0 hours

String Configuration (Voltage Calculations)

Voc(max) = Voc × [1 + βVoc × (Tmin − 25)] (β negative, so Voc rises when cold) Vmp(hot) = Vmp × [1 + βVmp × (Tcell,max − 25)] Vmp(cold) = Vmp × [1 + βVmp × (Tmin − 25)] Max modules = min( floor(Vinv,max / Voc(max)), floor(MPPTmax / Vmp(cold)) ) Min modules = ceil(MPPTmin / Vmp(hot)) Array current = 1.25 × Isc × parallel strings (AS/NZS 5033) ≤ inverter input rating Monthly generation: the location multipliers are normalised so the 12 months sum to the annual total.
Example: 30 kWh/day usage, Sydney location

Peak sun hours = 4.5h
System efficiency = 0.78
System size = 30 / (4.5 × 0.78) = 8.55 kW
Using 400W panels: 8550 / 400 = 21.4 → 22 panels
Final system: 22 × 400W = 8.8kW array
Inverter: 8.8kW × 0.9 = 7.9kW → 8kW inverter ✓

11. Fault Level Calculator

Standards Referenced:
  • IEC 60909 — Short-circuit currents in three-phase AC systems
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Clause 2.5.4–2.5.5
  • AS/NZS 3008.1.1 — Cable impedance data

Transformer Impedance (referred to LV)

Z_tx (Ω) = (%Z / 100) × V_LL² / S_VA R_tx = Z_tx / √(1 + (X/R)²) X_tx = (X/R) × R_tx

Upstream Network Impedance

Z_net = c_max × V_LL² / S_sc_VA (referred to LV side) Transformer correction (IEC 60909-0): K_T = 0.95 × c_max / (1 + 0.6 × x_T), Z_tx,K = K_T × Z_tx

Symmetrical 3-Phase Fault Current

I_3ph (kA) = c_max × V_LL / (√3 × Z_cumulative) c_max = 1.10 (LV, +10%) by default Peak factor (IEC 60909 Eq. 29): κ = 1.02 + 0.98 × e^(−3R/X) I_peak = κ × √2 × I_3ph

Single-Phase-to-Earth Fault

I_1ph,max = c_max × V_LN / Z_loop(20°C) I_1ph,min = c_min × V_LN / Z_loop(75°C), c_min = 0.95 — use for protective-device operation checks
Cable resistance (mΩ/m at 20°C — IEC 60228; Al = Cu × 1.64; × 1.216 for 75°C)
1.5mm²: 12.1 Cu / 19.8 Al  |  25mm²: 0.727 Cu / 1.19 Al  |  300mm²: 0.0601 Cu / 0.0986 Al
Cable reactance (mΩ/m)
0.069–0.110 mΩ/m (trefoil/flat formation)

12. Earth Fault Loop Impedance

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Clause 5.7, 1.5.5.3, Tables 8.1/8.2, Appendix B
  • IEC 60228 — conductor resistance

Loop Impedance

Z_s = Z_e + (R_active + R_earth) × L / 1000 (Ω) R_T = R_20 × [1 + 0.00393 × (T − 20)] (Cu; Al = Cu × 1.64), T = 75, 45 or 20°C Prospective fault current: I_f = U_o / Z_s (U_o = 230 V) Compliance: Z_s ≤ Z_s,max (no further 80% factor — conductors are already taken hot)

Maximum Permitted Z_s (AS/NZS 3000 Table 8.1)

MCBs: Z_s,max = 230 / (m × I_n), mean instantaneous trip m = 4 (B), 7.5 (C), 12.5 (D) (Paragraph B4.5) gG fuses (tabulated), 0.4 s: 6 A 11.5 | 10 A 6.4 | 16 A 3.1 | 20 A 2.1 | 25 A 1.6 | 32 A 1.3 | 40 A 1.0 | 50 A 0.7 | 63 A 0.6 Ω gG fuses (tabulated), 5 s: 6 A 15.3 | 10 A 9.2 | 16 A 5.0 | 20 A 3.6 | 25 A 2.7 | 32 A 2.2 | 40 A 1.6 | 50 A 1.3 | 63 A 0.9 Ω Measured test value (Table 8.2 basis): R_phe ≈ 0.8 × (Z_s,max − Z_e) at ~20°C Earth-conductor solver: smallest earth size with Z_s ≤ Z_s,max; L_max = (Z_s,max − Z_e) × 1000 / (R_a + R_e)
Example: C20 MCB, Ze = 0.35 Ω, 2.5/2.5 mm² Cu, 30 m at 75°C: R = 7.41 × 1.216 = 9.01 mΩ/m per conductor; Zs = 0.35 + 18.02 × 30/1000 = 0.891 Ω ≤ 230/(7.5 × 20) = 1.533 Ω ✓

13. Discrimination (Selectivity) Check

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Clause 2.5
  • IEC 60898-1 / IEC 60947-2 — circuit-breaker trip characteristics
  • IEC 60269 — gG fuse selectivity ratio

Overload Discrimination

Ratio = upstream I_r / downstream I_r (MCCB I_r = setting × I_n) ≥ 2.0 — likely OK | 1.6–2.0 — marginal, check curves | < 1.6 — unlikely

Short-Circuit Discrimination

Instantaneous bands: B 3–5 × I_n, C 5–10 × I_n, D 10–20 × I_n, MCCB setting × I_n ± 20% Selectivity limit I_s ≈ lowest upstream instantaneous threshold Bands overlap → not selective Bands separate and I_k ≤ I_s → total selectivity at this fault level Bands separate and I_k > I_s → partial selectivity up to I_s (use manufacturer tables) gG fuse / gG fuse: ratio ≥ 1.6 → selective; fuse upstream of breaker: I²t comparison (indicative ≥ 2:1)
Note: This is an indicative screening check. Manufacturer selectivity tables or time-current/I²t curves are the definitive check.

14. Transformer Sizing

Standards Referenced:
  • AS 2374.1-2 — Power transformers
  • AS/NZS 60076 — Losses and efficiency
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Clause 4.14

Design Load

Design kVA = Total Load × Diversity Factor × (1 + Growth% / 100)

Temperature Derating

Dry-type: 1.0% per °C above 40°C ambient Oil (ONAN): 0.7% per °C above 40°C ambient

Secondary Full-Load Current

I_FLC (3-phase) = (kVA × 1000) / (V_LL × √3) I_FLC (1-phase) = (kVA × 1000) / V

Prospective Fault Current

I_fault = I_FLC / (Z_k / 100)

Estimated Losses (AS 2374.1)

No-load loss (W) ≈ kVA × (1.5 + kVA × 0.0005) Load loss (W) ≈ kVA × (5 + kVA × 0.002) Efficiency η = P_out / (P_out + Total Losses) × 100%
Typical %Z_k by rating
≤200 kVA: 4.0–4.5% (oil) / 4.5% (dry)  |  201–500 kVA: 4.5–5.0% / 5.0%  |  501–1000 kVA: 5.0–5.75% / 5.75%

15. Generator Sizing

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3010:2017 — Electrical installations — Generating sets
  • ISO 8528-1 — standby / prime / continuous rating definitions
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Clause 7.3

Site Derating (typical — use manufacturer curves)

Altitude: −3.5% per 300 m above 1000 m Temperature: −2% per 5.5°C above 40°C Duty factor: standby 1.0, prime 0.9, continuous 0.75 of the standby rating

Running Load and Voltage Dip

Steady state: S_gen ≥ S_running × (1 + growth) / (duty factor × derating) Dip for a step: dip = X'd × S_start / (S_gen,available + X'd × S_start) Dip criterion: S_gen ≥ X'd × S_start × (1 − D_max) / D_max / derating Starting kVA: DOL 6.5×, star-delta 2.5×, VSD 1.2×, UPS 1.5× running kVA (or user value)

Fuel

Fuel (L) = 0.27 L/kWh × running kW × autonomy hours (also reported at full rated output, 0.8 pf)
Example: largest step 382 kVA DOL start, X'd = 0.25, Dmax = 20%: Sgen ≥ 0.25 × 382 × 0.8 / 0.2 = 382 kVA → 400 kVA set; dip = 0.25×382/(400 + 95.5) = 19.3% ✓

16. UPS Sizing

Standards Referenced:
  • AS 62040 series — Uninterruptible power systems
  • AS 3011 / AS 2676 — secondary batteries in buildings
  • IEEE 485 — ageing and temperature correction concepts

UPS Rating

Design kW = load kW × (1 + growth); design kVA = design kW / load pf Required kVA = max(design kVA, design kW / UPS output pf) Modules: N = ceil(required kVA / module kVA); installed = N (single), N + 1, or 2N

Battery Capacity (C10 equivalent, indicative)

DC current = design kW / 0.94 × 1000 / V_DC Required Ah = DC current × hours / (rate factor × ageing × temperature × usable window) Rate factor (fraction of C10 available): VRLA 5 min 0.33, 10 min 0.45, 15 min 0.53, 30 min 0.65, 60 min 0.75, 120 min 0.85 Temperature: −1% per °C below 25°C (VRLA/NiCd); no credit above 25°C (life reduces instead) Li-ion usable window 0.9; recharge current = discharged Ah / recharge time × 1.1

Required Reactive Power

φ₁ = arccos(PF_existing), φ₂ = arccos(PF_target) Q_required (kVAr) = P_kW × (tan φ₁ − tan φ₂) kVA reduction = kW/PF_existing − kW/PF_target

Resonance Risk Check

Resonant harmonic order: h_r = √(S_sc_kVA / Q_c_kVAr) If h_r coincides with a characteristic harmonic (5th, 7th, 11th…) → detuned reactor required Detuning: h_r near 3rd: 14% reactor (tunes to 134 Hz) h_r near 5th: 7% reactor (tunes to 189 Hz) h_r near 7th: 5.67% reactor (tunes to 210 Hz) Steps: n = ceil(Q_required / step size) so the installed bank is never below the requirement

19. Busbar Sizing

Standards Referenced:

  • IEC 60865-1 — mechanical effects of short-circuit currents
  • IEC 60949 — adiabatic short-circuit temperature limits

Continuous Current Rating

I = I_base × k_material × √(ΔT / 30 K) × k_enclosure × n × k_proximity I_base: single bare bar on edge, free air, 30 K rise (e.g. 50×10: 852 A, 100×10: 1490 A) k_material: Cu 1.0, Al 0.78; k_enclosure: free air 1.0, ventilated 0.85, IP2X–4X 0.75, IP54+ 0.65 k_proximity per bar: 2 bars 0.87, 3 bars 0.80, 4 bars 0.73

Short-Circuit Withstand

I_cw = k × A / √t, k = K × √ln[(β + θ_f)/(β + θ_i)] Cu: K = 226, β = 234.5; Al: K = 148, β = 228; θ_i = operating temperature, θ_f = 200°C e.g. Cu from 70°C: k ≈ 135 A·√s/mm²

Electrodynamic Force and Support Span

i_p = n × I_k (n: 1.5 ≤5 kA, 1.7 ≤10 kA, 2.0 ≤20 kA, 2.1 ≤50 kA, 2.2 >50 kA) F = 0.866 × 0.2 × i_p²(kA) × L / a (N); σ = 0.73 × F × L / (8 Z), Z = n_bars × b × t² / 6 σ ≤ 1.5 × R_p0.2 (Cu 200 N/mm², Al 100 N/mm²) → L_max = √(1.5 R_p0.2 × 8Z / (0.73 F/L))

Losses and Voltage Drop

R = ρ20 [1 + α(θ − 20)] / A × 1.05 (AC); X = 2πf × 2×10⁻⁷ × ln(1.26a / 0.2235(b + t)) P = 3 I² R L; V_d = √3 I L (R cos φ + X sin φ)
Example: 1600 A, 36 kA / 1 s, Cu, 40°C ambient, 70°C max, IP2X–4X enclosure (0.75): k = 226√ln(434.5/304.5) = 134.8 A·√s/mm². 120×10: 1740 × 0.75 = 1305 A ✗; 160×10: 2220 × 0.75 = 1665 A ✓ and 134.8 × 1600 / 1000 = 216 kA ≥ 36 kA ✓ → 160×10 mm, one bar per phase

21. Earthing Calculator

Standards Referenced:
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 — Section 5
  • AS/NZS 1768, BS 7430 — electrode resistance formulas
  • ENA EG-0 — Power system earthing guide

Vertical Rod (Dwight)

R = (ρ / 2πL) × [ln(8L / d) − 1] ρ (Ω·m), L rod length (m), d diameter (m)

Rods in Line (BS 7430)

R_n = R_1 × (1 + λα) / n, α = ρ / (2π R_1 s), λ = 2 × (1/2 + 1/3 + … + 1/n) λ: 2 rods 1.00, 3 rods 1.67, 4 rods 2.17, 10 rods 3.86; rod-count solver = smallest n with R_n ≤ target

Horizontal Strip (Sunde/Dwight)

R = (ρ / πL) × [ln(2L / √(2 a h)) − 1], a = w/4 (equivalent radius), h = burial depth

Earth Grid (Laurent-Niemann)

R = ρ / (4r) + ρ / L_total, r = √(A/π) (= 0.443 ρ/√A + ρ/L)

Buried Plate (BS 7430)

R = (ρ / 8r) × [1 + r / (2.5h + r)], r = √(A/π)
Example: ρ = 100 Ω·m, 2.4 m × 15.9 mm rod: R = 100/(2π×2.4) × [ln(8×2.4/0.0159) − 1] = 6.63 × 6.10 = 40.4 Ω
